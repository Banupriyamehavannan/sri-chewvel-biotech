Tissue Culture Banana Cultivation Guide

To achieve superior quality and maximum yield, planting disease-free tissue culture banana plantlets from Sri Chewvel Biotech is the ideal choice.

Advantages of Tissue Culture Banana Plants:

  • Completely disease-free at the time of supply.
  • Uniform growth across fields leading to synchronized, high yield.
  • Vigorous root system enabling healthy growth.
  • Plantlets are available for planting throughout the entire year.

Available Varieties:

  • Grand Nain (G9)
  • Rasakadali (Yelakki)
  • Red Banana (Sevvaazhai)
  • Karpooravalli
  • Country Banana (Monthan)
  • Nendran (Regular)

Soil Requirement & Preparation:

Soil: Well-drained red soil and alluvial loamy soil with a pH ranging between 6.5 and 7.5 are best suitable. Avoid planting in alkaline, saline, or waterlogged soils.
Land Preparation: Deep ploughing 2 to 3 times to make soil fine and clump-free. Dig pits of size 1.5 ft Length x 1.5 ft Width x 1.5 ft Depth.

Intercultural Practices:

  • Remove weeds periodically.
  • Prune side suckers, dry leaves, and trash regularly to keep the farm clean.
  • During hot summer and cold winter seasons, cover banana bunches using bunch sleeves or dry banana leaves for protection.

Crop Protection (Pests & Diseases):

Pests:
  • Corm Weevil: Holes in corm and roots causing severe damage. Control: Mix 2.5 ml Chlorpyrifos 20 EC per liter of water and drench around the base of the plant.
  • Nematodes: Red/black streak lesions on roots and root galls. Control: Apply 10g Carbofuran 3G per plant during planting, followed by 20g at the 3rd and 6th months.
  • Thrips: Rough, dull, and rust-like patches on fruits and skin. Control: Spray Monocrotophos at 2.5 ml/L or Imidacloprid at 0.5 ml/L of water.
  • Stem Borer: Gum-like sap oozing from bored stem holes. Control: Inject 2.5 ml of Monocrotophos solution (150 ml pesticide in 350 ml water) into the stem using a stem injector.
Diseases:
  • Bacterial Corm Rot (Erwinia): Rotting corm with scorched leaves. Heart leaf dries up and pulls out easily. Control: Apply Trichoderma / Pseudomonas in soil. Planting in the evening during sunny seasons is recommended.
  • Sigatoka Leaf Spot: Oval/eye-shaped brown spots on leaves causing premature fruit ripening. Control: Spray Nativo (0.5 g/L), Roko (1.5 g/L), or Tilt (1 ml/L) + Roko (1 g/L) at first sight of spots.
  • Bunchy Top Virus: Stunted spear-like leaves bunched at the crown. Spread by aphids. Control: Plant tested, virus-free Sri Chewvel Biotech plantlets. Spray Imidacloprid (0.5 ml/L) to control aphids.
  • Mosaic Virus: Pale green or yellow streaks and patches on leaves. Control: Uproot and destroy infected plants. Avoid intercropping with tomato, pumpkin, or cucumber.

Harvesting:

Harvest can be done 100 to 110 days after the emergence of the banana bunch.

* Note: The above cultivation practices are meticulously formulated based on our research field trial results. However, actual requirements may vary based on location, variety, and planting season.

How we grow your plants

Four reasons growers choose tissue-cultured plantlets over field-collected suckers — each one built to remove a specific risk from your planting material.

Disease-free culture

Propagation starts from sterile tissue, not field suckers — so plantlets leave the lab free of Fusarium wilt and other soil-borne infections common to traditional planting material.

True-to-type cloning

Each plantlet is a genetic clone of a selected high-yield mother plant, so your field matures on a predictable schedule with even fruit size across every row.

Hardened for the field

Plants leave our polyhouse at roughly 30 cm tall with an established root system, already acclimatized — which shortens the time to establishment after planting.

Supply on your schedule

Our dual facilities in Hosur and Mandithoppu let us fulfil large commercial orders year-round, without the seasonal gaps that limit sucker-based nurseries.
